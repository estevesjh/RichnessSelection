\section*{Purpose}
This note describes the numerical recipes for the operator
\(\Pop[X]\) (Costanzi~2026 eq.~9, our main TeX
\texttt{costanzi2026\_sigma\_prj\_b\_eff.tex} \S2.5) as currently
implemented in the \texttt{RichnessSelection} Python package. Two
axes have to be handled carefully: the outer \(z\)-integral (the
photo-z support carries sharp features from the
\(\xinl\)-times-exclusion interaction near \(z\!=\!\zob\)) and the
inner \(\theta\)-integral (a hard step at the exclusion radius
\(R_{\rm excl}\) that moves with z). The \(\theta\)-handling turned
out to be the dominant precision driver: eliminating the
\(\theta\)-step via a per-z change of integration limits bought two
orders of magnitude in precision and reduced \(N_\theta\) from
\(\sim 120\) to \(\sim 10\). The \(z\)-axis recipe family (Options
D, E, and the new adaptive Option C) is documented once that
bottleneck is removed.

\section*{Production recipe at a glance}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\(\theta\)-axis}: \emph{split at the exclusion
        boundary} (\S\ref{sec:theta_split}). GL on
        \([\theta_{\rm excl}(z),\,2\theta_\lambda^{\rm tgt}]\), no mask.
        \(N_\theta = 10\) reaches sub-\(0.01\%\) on \(\Pop[1]\),
        \(I_1\), \(I_2\).
  \item \textbf{\(z\)-axis}: \emph{CDF-based adaptive grid}
        (Option~C, \S\ref{sec:optionC}). GL at uniform CDF levels
        built from a cheap rough proxy
        \(\bigl|f_{\Pop[1]}(z)\bigr|+\bigl|f_{I_2}(z)\bigr|\).
  \item \textbf{inner \((M,\lambda)\)}: GL with
        \(N_M\!=\!24\), \(N_\lambda\!=\!60\); both already converged
        at these sizes.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Total cost at \((\lob,\zob)=(20,0.5)\), \(N_z=80\),
\(N_\theta=10\): \(\sim 26\) ms per \texttt{bias\_precompute}
with Options~D or E, \(\sim 220\) ms with Option~C (due to the
rough-grid setup cost).

\section{The \(\theta\)-axis: split at the exclusion boundary}
\label{sec:theta_split}

The \(\theta\)-integrand for \(I_1\) and \(I_2\) contains the
product \(\xinl(\Delta\chi(z,\theta))\cdot f_A(\theta)\cdot
\sin\theta\) with the halo-exclusion prescription
\(\xinl\!\equiv\!0\) for
\(\Delta\chi\!<\!R_{\rm excl}\!=\!R_\lambda(\lob)(1+\zob)\).
At any \(z\) the exclusion kills all \(\theta\) below a threshold
\(\theta_{\rm excl}(z)\) obtained by solving
\(\Delta\chi(z,\theta_{\rm excl})=R_{\rm excl}\):
\begin{equation}
\cos\theta_{\rm excl}(z)
\;=\;
\frac{\chi(z)^2+\chi(\zob)^2-R_{\rm excl}^2}
     {2\,\chi(z)\,\chi(\zob)},
\qquad
\text{clipped to }[-1,1].
\label{eq:theta_excl_def}
\end{equation}
If the right-hand side is \(\geq 1\), no \(\theta\) is excluded at
this \(z\) (this is the case when
\(|\Delta\chi_\parallel|>R_{\rm excl}\)). Otherwise
\(\theta_{\rm excl}(z)\in(0,\pi)\) and the integrand has a
\emph{hard step} at that \(\theta\).

\paragraph{Why a fixed-\(\theta\) GL grid fails.}
With \(N_\theta=20\) uniform GL nodes on \((0,2\theta_\lambda^{\rm tgt})\)
and an exclusion mask \(\xinl\!\to\!0\) inside the step, the
numerical integrand is a step function. GL polynomial-fits the
step and returns an error that oscillates with \(N_\theta\). At the
reference point, we measured:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{r r r}
\toprule
\(N_\theta\) & \(I_2\) rel.\ err & \(I_1\) rel.\ err \\
\midrule
10  & \(-1.33\%\) & \(-1.62\%\) \\
15  & \(+2.40\%\) & \(+2.53\%\) \\
20  & \(-0.56\%\) & \(-0.57\%\) \\
50  & \(-0.42\%\) & \(-0.45\%\) \\
80  & \(+0.38\%\) & \(+0.42\%\) \\
120 & \(-0.01\%\) & \(+0.01\%\) \\
200 & \(+0.18\%\) & \(+0.20\%\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
There is a 0.3\% noise floor that does not decrease with
\(N_\theta\). That is the classical signature of integrating across
a discontinuity with a fixed-rule method.

\paragraph{The fix.}
Per-\(z\) change of the \emph{integration interval}: when
\(\theta_{\rm excl}(z)>0\), integrate on
\([\theta_{\rm excl}(z),\,2\theta_\lambda^{\rm tgt}]\). No mask. The
GL nodes now cluster at the endpoints (as the Gauss rule naturally
does), the integrand is smooth on the interval because the
exclusion step coincides with the lower endpoint, and GL converges
at its design rate:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{r r r r}
\toprule
\(N_\theta\) & \(\Pop[1]\) err & \(I_2\) err & \(I_1\) err \\
\midrule
5   & \(+0.060\%\) & \(+0.046\%\) & \(+0.046\%\) \\
8   & \(+0.018\%\) & \(+0.005\%\) & \(+0.016\%\) \\
\textbf{10}  & \(\mathbf{-0.005\%}\) & \(\mathbf{-0.001\%}\) & \(\mathbf{-0.004\%}\) \\
15  & \(+0.000\%\) & \(+0.000\%\) & \(+0.000\%\) \\
20+ & \(\approx 0\) & \(\approx 0\) & \(\approx 0\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\(N_\theta=10\) is converged to sub-\(0.01\%\) and \(N_\theta=15\) is
bit-exact at 5 digits. This is a \(\sim 12\times\) reduction in
\(\theta\)-nodes relative to the mask-based recipe while reaching
\emph{two orders of magnitude better} precision on \(I_1, I_2\).

\paragraph{Implementation note.}
Because \(\theta_{\rm excl}(z)\) is \(z\)-dependent, the \(\theta\)
integration is now inside a per-\(z\) Python loop in
\texttt{sel\_bias.py::\_P\_operator} rather than a single
broadcast-vectorised einsum over \((N_\theta,N_\lambda,N_M,N_z)\).
The per-\(z\) loop adds only \(\sim 6\) ms at \(N_z=80\); the
remaining contractions over \((M,\lambda)\) still use einsum.
This is a favourable trade: three orders of magnitude precision
for a 25\% time penalty, and \(N_\theta\) can be halved which
buys some of that back.

\noindent\textit{The rest of this note assumes
split-at-exclusion is applied. All z-axis recipes (D, E, C)
below use it.}

\section{The integrand \(f(z)\)}

Any of the three operators \(\Pop[1], I_1, I_2\) factorises as
\begin{equation}
I = \int dz\,f(z)\,,
\qquad
f(z)\;\equiv\;\frac{dV}{dz\,d\Omega}(z)\,w_z(z,\zob)\,
     \mathcal{I}_{\rm inner}(z)\,,
\label{eq:fz_factorisation}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{I}_{\rm inner}(z)\) is the triple
\((M,\lambda,\theta)\) integral at this \(z\), defined explicitly
as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{I}_{\rm inner}(z)
\;\equiv\;
\int d\ln M\;M\,n(M,z)\,\mathcal{B}(M,z)
\int d\lambda\,P(\lambda|M,z)\,\lambda
\cdot 2\pi\!\int d\theta\,\sin\theta\,
f_A(\theta,\lambda,z)\,\mathcal{X}(z,\theta).
\label{eq:I_inner_def}
\end{equation}
The two per-operator factors are
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
operator & \(\mathcal{B}(M,z)\) & \(\mathcal{X}(z,\theta)\) \\
\midrule
\(\Pop[1]\) & \(1\) & \(1\) \\
\(I_2\)     & \(b(M,z)\) & \(\xinl\!\bigl(\Delta\chi(z,\theta)\bigr)\) \\
\(I_1\)     & \(b(M,z)\) &
                \(\xinl\!\bigl(\Delta\chi(z,\theta)\bigr)\,\sigma(\theta)\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
with \(\sigma(\theta)=[1+e^{-k(\theta-\theta_0)}]^{-1}\) the
large/small-scale transition sigmoid (\(k=2.5/\theta_\lambda^{\rm tgt}\),
\(\theta_0=\theta_\lambda^{\rm tgt}/2\)) and \(\Delta\chi\) the 3-D
comoving separation
\[
\Delta\chi(z,\theta)=
\sqrt{\bigl(\chi(z)-\chi(\zob)\bigr)^2+\chi(\zob)^2\theta^2}\;.
\]
Halo exclusion enforces \(\xinl\equiv 0\) for
\(\Delta\chi<R_{\rm excl}\equiv R_\lambda(\lob)\,(1+\zob)\),
which is the source of the sharp features discussed below. For
\(\Pop[1]\) there is no \(\xinl\) and no exclusion structure, so
\(f(z)\) is smooth: \(\Pop[1]\) is not the hard part.

\paragraph{Characteristic scales.}
At \(\lob\!=\!20\), \(\zob\!=\!0.5\) (Buzzard v1.1 cosmology):
\begin{align}
R_{\rm excl} &= R_\lambda(\lob)\,(1+\zob) \approx 1.09\,\hMpc,\\
c/H(\zob) &= d\chi/dz\big|_{\zob} \approx 2311\,\hMpc,\\
\delta z_{\rm excl} &\equiv \frac{R_{\rm excl}}{c/H(\zob)}
                     \approx 4.7\times 10^{-4}.
\end{align}
\(\delta z_{\rm excl}\) is the \(z\)-half-width of the ``exclusion
\(z\)-band'' around \(\zob\): for
\(|z-\zob|<\delta z_{\rm excl}\), even the \(\theta\!=\!0\)
component of the 3-D separation is zero, and exclusion zeros the
small-\(\theta\) part of the integrand. But \emph{exclusion is on
the 3-D separation, not on \(|\Delta\chi_\parallel|\)} --- the
large-\(\theta\) part (\(\theta>R_{\rm excl}/\chi(\zob)\)) always
has \(\Delta\chi>R_{\rm excl}\) and contributes a nonzero ``ring''
term.

\paragraph{Three regions.}
The integrand \(f(z)\) (Fig.~\ref{fig:fz}) has three
qualitatively distinct regions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[{\bf R1.}]
    \textbf{Ring plateau} \(|z-\zob|<\delta z_{\rm excl}\). The
    small-\(\theta\) part is killed by exclusion; only
    \(\theta>R_{\rm excl}/\chi(\zob)\) contributes, through the
    ``ring'' of projected structures at 3-D distance
    \(>R_{\rm excl}\). \(f(z)\) sits near a
    \emph{flat plateau} of \(\sim\!60\) at the reference point.

  \item[{\bf R2.}]
    \textbf{Exclusion peaks}. Right outside the exclusion band,
    at \(|z-\zob|\simeq\delta z_{\rm excl}\), \(\xinl\) is evaluated
    at \(\Delta\chi\!\to\!R_{\rm excl}\) where
    \(\xinl\propto\Delta\chi^{-1.7}\) is maximal. \(f(z)\) has two
    sharp peaks, symmetrically placed at
    \(z=\zob\pm\delta z_{\rm excl}\), with amplitude
    \(\sim\!170\) --- roughly three times the plateau.

  \item[{\bf R3.}]
    \textbf{Outer power-law decay}. For
    \(|z-\zob|\gg\delta z_{\rm excl}\) the integrand falls off
    smoothly as \(\xinl\propto\Delta\chi_\parallel^{-1.7}\) modulated
    by \(w_z(z,\zob)\) which truncates at the photo-z kernel
    support \(|z-\zob|\simeq\sigma_z(\zob)\approx 0.09\).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Option E: split by physics}
\label{sec:optionE}

Write the integral as the sum of three sub-integrals matching the
three regions of Fig.~\ref{fig:fz}:
\begin{equation}
I \;=\; I_{\rm ring} \;+\; I_{\rm outer}^{\rm fg}
    \;+\; I_{\rm outer}^{\rm bg}
\,,
\end{equation}
and integrate each on a grid tailored to its integrand shape.

\subsection*{R1: ring plateau \(I_{\rm ring}\) --- Gauss--Legendre
in \(z\)}
Interval: \((z_{\rm ring,lo},z_{\rm ring,hi})
= (\zob-\delta z_{\rm excl},\,\zob+\delta z_{\rm excl})\).
The integrand is smooth (no \(\xinl\) variation in this band,
because the small-\(\theta\) part is excluded everywhere in R1).
Gauss--Legendre in \(z\) with \(N_{\rm ring}\sim 10\)--\(20\) nodes
is optimal: smooth integrand, fixed interval, polynomial-exact for
\(\deg\le 2N_{\rm ring}-1\).

\subsection*{R2+R3: outer regions --- GL in
\(u=\ln|\Delta\chi_\parallel|\)}
Foreground interval:
\(|\Delta\chi_\parallel|\in(R_{\rm excl},\chi(\zob)-\chi(z^{\rm fg}_{\min}))\).
Background interval:
\(|\Delta\chi_\parallel|\in(R_{\rm excl},\chi(z^{\rm bg}_{\max})-\chi(\zob))\).
Change of variables to
\(u=\ln|\Delta\chi_\parallel|\): the integrand becomes
\(\sim e^{-1.7 u}\) near the inner edge (peak at
\(u=\ln R_{\rm excl}\) because
\(\xinl\propto e^{-1.7u}\) is maximal there) and a smooth
decaying tail further out. Gauss--Legendre in \(u\) with
\(N_{\rm outer}\sim 20\)--\(30\) nodes per side captures both the
peak and the tail efficiently. The Jacobian for the final z-integral is
\begin{equation}
\frac{dz}{du}\;=\;\frac{|dz/d\chi|\cdot|d\chi/du|}{1}
             \;=\;\frac{|\Delta\chi_\parallel|}{c/H(z)}
\,,
\end{equation}
which multiplies each GL weight before the integrand is evaluated.

\subsection*{Why this helps}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Each region's integrand is smooth in its own
        variable.} The three singular/non-smooth structures of
        Fig.~\ref{fig:fz} (band boundaries, exclusion peaks,
        power-law tails) are all \emph{located exactly on the
        region boundaries}, where no node sits, so polynomial GL
        rules converge at their design rate.
  \item \textbf{No log-spike missing.} The plateau in R1 is
        captured by uniform-in-\(z\) GL; the peaks in R2 sit
        \emph{at the boundary} \(u=\ln R_{\rm excl}\) of the GL
        interval (GL nodes cluster near endpoints
        \(\propto 1/\sqrt{1-x^2}\), which is where we want them).
  \item \textbf{Convergence is monotonic in \(N\) per region.} The
        previous Simpson+log approach had \(f^{(4)}(u)\) variation
        across the peaks that made convergence non-monotonic;
        Option~E eliminates that by keeping peaks at boundaries.
\end{itemize}

The residual \(\sim 0.5\)--\(0.9\%\) on \(I_1,I_2\) is

itself is converged at \(N_z\!=\!80\).

\paragraph{Edge cases.}
If \(R_{\rm excl}\) exceeds the photo-z support on either side
(very tight photo-z or very large \(\lob\)), the outer region on
that side is empty and the code skips it. If the ring band
\([\zob-\delta z_{\rm excl},\zob+\delta z_{\rm excl}]\) is wider
than the photo-z support (very small \(\lob\), very loose
photo-z), it is clipped to the support. Both edge cases are
handled by \texttt{np.clip} / empty-array guards in the
implementation (\texttt{sel\_bias.py::\_P\_operator}).

\section{Option D: arcsinh transform for \(I_1\), \(I_2\)}
\label{sec:optionD}

Option~E treats the ring-plateau (R1) and the outer regions (R2+R3)
separately because they have different variables in which they are
naturally smooth. Option~D makes an alternative bet: a single
change-of-variable that smooths the full domain.

\paragraph{The transform.}
Let \(R_{\rm excl}\equiv R_\lambda(\lob)(1+\zob)\) and define
\begin{equation}
t \;\equiv\; \mathrm{asinh}\!\left(\frac{|\Delta\chi_\parallel|}{R_{\rm excl}}\right),
\qquad
\frac{d|\Delta\chi_\parallel|}{dt} \;=\; R_{\rm excl}\,\cosh t \,.
\label{eq:asinh_transform}
\end{equation}
The key properties of the transform at the three characteristic
scales of Fig.~\ref{fig:fz}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item At \(|\Delta\chi_\parallel|\!\to\!0\) (centre of R1):
        \(t\!\to\!0\), \(\cosh t\!\to\!1\), the ring plateau is
        mapped to a small-\(t\) neighbourhood with bounded
        Jacobian.
  \item At \(|\Delta\chi_\parallel|\!=\!R_{\rm excl}\) (peak of R2):
        \(t\!=\!\mathrm{asinh}(1)\!\approx\!0.881\), a universal
        fixed value independent of cosmology. \(\xinl\) is near
        maximum but the Jacobian
        \(\cosh t\!\approx\!\sqrt 2\!\approx\!1.41\) is bounded, so
        the peak becomes a smooth local maximum of the
        \(t\)-integrand.
  \item At \(|\Delta\chi_\parallel|\!\gg\!R_{\rm excl}\):
        \(t\!\to\!\ln(2|\Delta\chi_\parallel|/R_{\rm excl})\), so
        the transform is asymptotically logarithmic --- same as
        Option~E's outer piece \(u=\ln|\Delta\chi_\parallel|\). In
        particular the \(\xinl\propto r^{-1.7}\) power law times
        the \(\cosh t\) Jacobian gives a smooth exponential decay
        \(\sim\!e^{-0.7\,t}\) at large \(t\).
\end{itemize}
A single Gauss--Legendre grid in \(t\in[0,\,t_{\max}]\) per side
of \(\zob\) (where
\(t_{\max}=\mathrm{asinh}(|\Delta\chi_\parallel^{\max}|/R_{\rm excl})\))
therefore covers ring plateau, exclusion peak, and outer decay in
one pass without a region split. The Jacobian for the \(z\)-integral
is
\begin{equation}
\frac{dz}{dt}
\;=\;\frac{|d\chi/dt|}{|d\chi/dz|}
\;=\;\frac{R_{\rm excl}\cosh t}{c/H(z)}\,.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Why this helps.}
The \(\xinl\!\propto\!r^{-1.7}\) near-exclusion divergence,
multiplied by the \(\cosh t\) Jacobian, yields a bounded smooth
integrand of \(t\) near \(t\!\approx\!0.881\). The small-\(t\)
plateau contribution and the large-\(t\) exponential tail are also
smooth. No sharp feature remains in the \(t\)-variable, so GL
converges faster per node than Option~E on those axes where
Option~E keeps a region split.

\paragraph{Why not make Option~D the default.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Kernel-specific.} The transform is tuned to the
        \(\xinl\sim r^{-1.7}\) near-exclusion behaviour via the
        universal pivot \(R_{\rm excl}\). If \(\xinl\) is replaced
        by a linear \(\xi\), a different halofit prescription, or
        an emulator, the location of the peak in
        \(t\)-coordinates shifts and node density is no longer
        optimal at \(t\!=\!0.881\). Option~E makes no such
        assumption.
  \item \textbf{\(\Pop[1]\) has no \(\xinl\),} so the transform's
        smoothing is wasted there. Option~E's region split
        correctly recognises that \(\Pop[1]\) is smooth
        everywhere and uses GL directly.
  \item \textbf{Non-monotonic convergence.} Both schemes oscillate,
        but D's oscillation amplitude is larger at low \(N_z\)
        (\(\sim\!2\%\) at \(N_z\!=\!40\)) than E's
        (\(\sim\!0.8\%\)). Option~E is more forgiving of a
        user choosing a tight \(N_z\) for speed.
  \item \textbf{Option~E matches Matteo's notebook structure.}
        His \texttt{numerator2}/\texttt{int\_over\_phi4} follow the
        log-spaced LoS pattern; Option~E is a drop-in upgrade.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{When to use Option~D.}
When MCMC runtime is dominated by \(I_1,I_2\) and the user has
already established \(N_z\!\geq\!60\) via convergence testing,
Option~D gives a \(\sim\!2\times\) reduction in residual error at
the same node count. In
\texttt{RichnessSelection} it is enabled via
\texttt{SelBias(..., z\_scheme='D')}.

\section{Option C: CDF-based adaptive \(z\)-grid}
\label{sec:optionC}

Both Options D and E are \emph{fixed-rule} methods: they place GL
nodes at the same locations regardless of the integrand shape,
parametrized only by \(R_{\rm excl}\) and the photo-z support
endpoints. Option C is \emph{adaptive}: it uses the actual
integrand values on a cheap rough grid to decide where to place
the final GL nodes.

\paragraph{The recipe.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Build a rough \(z\)-grid of
        \(N_{\rm rough}=200\) log-spaced nodes per side of \(\zob\),
        i.e.\ 400 nodes total covering
        \([z^{\rm fg}_{\min},z^{\rm bg}_{\max}]\) densely around the
        exclusion band.
  \item Evaluate a cheap proxy for \(|f(z)|\) at each rough node.
        We use
        \[
        f_{\rm proxy}(z) \;\equiv\;
        \bigl|f_{\Pop[1]}(z)\bigr| \;+\;
        \bigl|f_{I_2}(z)\bigr|,
        \]
        the sum of absolute values of the
        \(\Pop[1]\)- and \(I_2\)-integrands (same inner \((M,\lambda,\theta)\)
        grids as the final integration). Summing them ensures the
        CDF places nodes around both distributional features:
        \(\Pop[1]\) peaks at \(z\!=\!\zob\) (smooth core),
        \(I_2\) peaks at
        \(z\!=\!\zob\!\pm\!\delta z_{\rm excl}\) (exclusion peaks).
  \item Cumulative-trapezoidal integrate \(f_{\rm proxy}\) in \(z\),
        normalise to
        \(F(z)\in[0,1]\).
  \item Take \(N_z\) GL nodes \(\{u_i,w_i\}\) on \([0,1]\). Invert
        the CDF:
        \(z_i=F^{-1}(u_i)\) via \texttt{np.interp}. The Jacobian
        \(dz/du=\text{total}/|f_{\rm proxy}(z_i)|\) multiplies each
        GL weight. Evaluate the real (signed) integrand at the
        \(z_i\) nodes.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Why it works.}
GL with a fixed interval places \(\sim 1/\sqrt{1-x^2}\) nodes at
the endpoints of its canonical \([-1,1]\) range. Mapping
\(x\to z\) via the rough CDF means nodes cluster where
\(|f_{\rm proxy}(z)|\) is large, i.e.\ around the exclusion peaks.
In effect, the scheme \emph{discovers} the exclusion-peak structure
of \(f(z)\) (which Option E hand-codes) and also covers whatever
\(z\)-feature a different cosmology / richness bin might introduce,
without any manual tuning.

\paragraph{When C wins vs when E wins.}
Once \(\theta\) is split at the exclusion boundary, the
\(z\)-axis is already well-behaved and \textbf{Option~E at
\(N_z=80\), \(N_\theta=10\) reaches sub-\(0.01\%\) on all three

(\(\sim 0.6\%\) vs E's \(0.003\%\)) because the proxy
\(|f_{\Pop[1]}|+|f_{I_2}|\) does not include the sigmoid weighting
that defines \(I_1\), so its CDF misplaces nodes for \(I_1\)
specifically. C also costs \(\sim 8\times\) more due to the rough-grid
setup (400 inner-integrand evaluations per
\texttt{bias\_precompute}).

\paragraph{When to pick C.}
Option~C is useful when the integrand family is not known in
advance or changes between runs (e.g.\ testing a new \(\xinl\)
prescription, switching from DES Y3 to a different survey with a
different photo-z kernel, or diagnosing where precision is being
lost). For those ``first-look'' situations, C's automatic
node-placement is robust even though it costs more per call. For
production MCMC, Option~E remains the default because it is faster
and equally accurate on the integrals that matter.

In \texttt{RichnessSelection}, Option~C is enabled via
\texttt{SelBias(..., z\_scheme='C')}. See
\texttt{src/richness\_selection/sel\_bias.py::\_z\_grid\_option\_C}
for the implementation.

\section{Implementation notes}

All three \(z\)-schemes (D, E, C) and the split-at-exclusion
\(\theta\)-recipe are implemented in
\texttt{src/richness\_selection/sel\_bias.py}. Dispatch via the
constructor argument \texttt{z\_scheme}
(\texttt{'E'}, \texttt{'D'}, or \texttt{'C'}).

Option~E, \texttt{\_z\_grid\_option\_E}, splits
\(N_z\) as \(N_{\rm ring}=\max(9,\,N_z/4)\) and
\(N_{\rm outer}=\max(15,(N_z-N_{\rm ring})/2)\) per side, GL on
\(\ln|\Delta\chi_\parallel|\) for the outer halves.

Option~D, \texttt{\_z\_grid\_option\_D}, uses
\(N_{\rm side}=\max(15,\,N_z/2)\) GL nodes per foreground/background
side in \(t=\mathrm{asinh}(|\Delta\chi_\parallel|/R_{\rm excl})\),
with no region split.

Option~C, \texttt{\_z\_grid\_option\_C}, builds a 400-node rough
\(z\)-grid (log-spaced on each side of \(\zob\)), evaluates
\(f_{\rm proxy}(z)=|f_{\Pop[1]}(z)|+|f_{I_2}(z)|\) at each node,
cumulative-integrates to get a CDF, and places the final \(N_z\)
GL nodes at uniform-CDF levels via
\texttt{np.interp}.

The \(\theta\)-axis (split at
\(\theta_{\rm excl}(z)\)) is inside a per-\(z\) loop in
\texttt{\_P\_operator}; the \((M,\lambda)\) contractions are still
numpy-einsum. Default grid sizes
(\texttt{GridConfig}): \(N_z\!=\!80\), \(N_\theta\!=\!10\),
\(N_M\!=\!24\), \(N_\lambda\!=\!60\).

Regression tests live in the \texttt{RichnessSelection} package:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{validations/theta\_convergence.py}: sweeps
        \(N_\theta\) at fixed \(N_z\!=\!80\) showing that
        \(N_\theta\!=\!10\) reaches sub-\(0.01\%\) with
        split-at-exclusion.
  \item \texttt{validations/quad\_matched\_inner.py}: compares E
        at \(N_z\!=\!80\) to
        \texttt{scipy.integrate.quad} (rtol\,=\,\(10^{-5}\)) using
        the same inner GL grids; must pass \(<\!0.1\%\) on
        \(\Pop[1], I_1, I_2\).
  \item \texttt{validations/compare\_schemes.py}: prints the
        E/D/C comparison table above at the reference point and
        across six \((\lob,\zob)\) bins.
\end{itemize}

For typical DES Y3 cluster redshift bins
(\(\zob\in[0.1,0.8]\), \(\lob\in[20,500]\)) no retuning of the
grid-size defaults should be needed: the \emph{geometry} of
Fig.~\ref{fig:fz} is universal in shape, only \(R_{\rm excl}\) and
\(c/H(\zob)\) shift with cosmology/richness, and all three schemes
(and the split-at-exclusion \(\theta\)-recipe) track those scales
analytically.
